A Hundred Years to Freedom

The count

You walk a brick block, a guarded corridor, a yard, a mess hall, and the showers. The camera stays above you. Talk only when you are close enough to touch someone. Everyone has something to say, even if it is only “keep walking.”

Twenty errands loop. An officer’s job takes one year off the sentence and costs two respect. A job from another inmate adds one year and pays two respect. Respect stops at one hundred. Years can climb or fall. Nothing in the block is a weapon, and there is no escape to win.

Administration

Scrub a bowl. Mop an aisle. File a slip. Carry a name back to the warden. The book gets lighter. The yard likes you less.

The yard

Pass a word, find a letter, do honest reps, keep a bench quiet. The sentence grows. Your name in here grows with it.

On the phone

The Android app is offline. Progress stays on the device: years left, respect, and which errand you are on. The package is com.hundredyears.freedom. Cell doors open when you press the action button, then shut themselves after two seconds, from either side of the bars.
